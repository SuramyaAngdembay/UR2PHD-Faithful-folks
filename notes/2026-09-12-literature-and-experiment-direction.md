# Literature assessment and experimental direction for CoT faithfulness

The strongest direction for this project is to establish which observable properties a faithfulness detector measures, and when those properties transfer across datasets. The literature supports that question, but substantially reduces the novelty of simply showing that evaluators disagree. A convincing next contribution would connect the existing transfer failure to independently checked evidence on common examples, then validate the resulting explanation in a fresh setting.

This assessment distinguishes published results, completed project findings, and proposed experiments. The current evidence does not establish a successful corrective method or a general two-regime law. The completed project campaign remains 226 API calls; the experiments proposed below are not newly completed results. The literature and release status were checked on September 12, 2026.

**Corrections to the proposed interpretation.** The acronym distinction between the two GRACE papers is correct. Khalifa et al.'s decoding method uses a discriminator to select reasoning steps. Its training labels involve aligning generated trajectories with reference reasoning, so describing its entire target as mechanically verified overstates the procedure. It offers a useful correctness-oriented comparison and a lesson about realistic negatives; its success does not itself demonstrate a separation between correctness and faithfulness. Pham et al.'s GRACE benchmark is the relevant released step-level evaluation resource. [GRACE decoding](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14934), [GRACE benchmark](https://github.com/pvhoang14/GRACE-benchmark).[^1][^2]

Young's classifier-sensitivity paper is a closer novelty overlap than the proposed assessment suggests. It already reports ranking reversals, alongside up to 30.6 percentage-point differences on identical traces. The comparison changes criteria such as lexical mention versus substantive acknowledgment; it is not just interchangeable classifiers approximating one fixed rule. Its lack of an independent reference also limits the inference that disagreement proves construct divergence rather than classification error. Our distinction must rest on identifying and validating a transfer failure, not on claiming to discover evaluator-dependent rankings. [Young, full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20172).[^3]

Zaman and Srivastava contest treating missing hint acknowledgment as sufficient evidence of unfaithfulness. Their approximately 90% result is faithful@k: obtaining an acknowledgment across multiple attempts, including a reported setting at 16 samples. It is not a 90% per-response rate established by lengthening each response. Their alternative measures and mediation analysis also do not independently certify every claim in a trace. The paper motivates separating disclosure from causal influence; it does not validate every omitted disclosure or invalidate BonaFide's entire target. [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23032).[^4]

There is a simple mathematical reason to separate the two budget claims. Under independent attempts with a fixed per-attempt acknowledgment probability p, the probability of at least one acknowledgment is 1 − (1 − p)^k. A success rate of 0.9 at k = 16 is compatible with an unchanged per-attempt probability of approximately 0.134. This calculation is illustrative, not a fitted explanation of the paper's results; heterogeneous examples and correlated attempts change the curve. Nevertheless, increasing best-of-k success alone cannot establish that a longer individual explanation resolves an omission.

Two project-specific corrections are equally important. The common FaithCoT comparison gives the judge approximately .771 AUROC versus .696 for the incorrectness oracle; the oracle does not beat every purpose-built detector. BonaFide's whole-label rule also includes explicit unfaithful steps and missing required steps, alongside acknowledgment-related failures. It is not reproduced by a missing-hint-words rule. The existing audit identifies overlapping reason flags; they must not be treated as mutually exclusive classes. [Current evidence](../CURRENT_STATE.md), [completed diagnostics](2026-09-12-autonomous-experiment-results.md).

The possibility that a judge follows a defensible alternative criterion remains worth testing. It cannot be inferred from below-chance native-label performance. A judge could be applying another criterion, misreading the prompt, making unsupported allegations, or responding to population differences. Some of our inspected rationales contain claims contradicted by the actual input, which an alternative definition does not excuse.

**Research that provides concrete experimental guidance.** The following papers matter for different reasons. Some are already in the bibliography; the recommendation is to use their experimental logic more directly.

| Work | Relevant contribution and limitation | Consequence for this project |
|---|---|---|
| [A Causal Lens for Evaluating Faithfulness Metrics](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1496/) — Zaman and Srivastava, EMNLP 2025[^9] | Uses interventions that preserve an answer while changing its explanatory basis. The main setup uses in-context editing, with a parametric-edit comparison; successful editing and plausible counterfactuals require checking. | Strongest immediate design inspiration: test whether detectors recognize the appropriate explanation when correctness cannot distinguish the pair. Public [code and data structures](https://github.com/KeremZaman/CausalDiagnosticity) provide a starting point. |
| [NSF-CoT](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.516/) — Pramanik et al., Findings of ACL 2026[^10] | Combines formalized claims, context attribution, and hybrid verification. Parsing, attribution quality, and fallback behavior constrain what the resulting scores establish. | A close comparator for any claim–evidence verification method. A component rubric alone is not a sufficient methodological contribution. |
| [Faithfulness as Information Flow](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24286v1) — Jia, Benton, and Easley, 2026[^11] | Distinguishes information-flow properties including necessity, sufficiency, and completeness. These concern model dependence, not the truth of every textual assertion. | Use explicit causal endpoints and compare them with source-claim truth on the same examples. Re-encoding a trace in a different reader does not establish the original generator's information flow. |
| [On Measuring Faithfulness or Self-consistency of Natural Language Explanations](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.329/) — Parcalabescu and Frank, ACL 2024[^12] | Distinguishes perturbation-based consistency from faithfulness; introduces CC-SHAP comparisons of input contributions. | Revisit step-removal conclusions with controls for re-solving and input changes. A changed answer is not a complete explanation of the generating mechanism. |
| [Measuring Chain of Thought Faithfulness by Unlearning Reasoning Steps](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14829v4) — Tutek et al., EMNLP 2025[^13] | FUR uses targeted parameter changes to probe dependence. Unlearning efficacy, specificity, and collateral effects remain relevant. | An optional targeted mechanistic check; expensive intervention does not eliminate the need for controls. |
| [Counterfactual Simulation Training for CoT Faithfulness](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20710) — Hase and Potts, 2026[^14] | Tests and trains traces for predicting counterfactual model behavior, with important cue-dependent transfer limitations. | Borrow the evaluation comparison with and without the trace and reserve new cue families. Starting a training project now would add scope before the target is resolved. |
| [CoT Reasoning in the Wild Is Not Always Faithful](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08679) — Arcuschin et al.[^15] | Studies naturally occurring reasoning failures through controlled question changes, rather than relying entirely on injected hints. | Useful inspiration for an eventual cue-free external check; different intervention assumptions still need to be declared. |

These precedents also make a broad conceptual claim difficult to sell as new. The distinction between a plausible explanation and a faithful one predates the current CoT benchmarks. The opportunity is a well-identified empirical result and a useful evaluation procedure, rather than a new vocabulary for already recognized differences. [Jacovi and Goldberg, ACL 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.386/).[^5]

**What another dataset would actually add.** Dataset count is a poor proxy for identification. Each addition should supply a missing observable or comparison that the current corpora cannot provide.

| Resource | Availability checked | Useful endpoint | What it cannot establish by itself |
|---|---|---|---|
| [GRACE benchmark](https://github.com/pvhoang14/GRACE-benchmark) | Full test release archived: 437 traces, 2,044 steps, four tasks | Context grounding, inference errors, and localization under human step labels | Whether the original model internally depended on the displayed reasoning |
| [FACE-Eval](https://huggingface.co/datasets/edinburgh-dawg/face-eval) and [released model outputs](https://huggingface.co/datasets/edinburgh-dawg/face-eval-results) | Public artifacts listed; not all trajectories downloaded | Cue adoption and disclosure across presentation conditions | Comprehensive truth of the narrated process |
| [CausalDiagnosticity](https://github.com/KeremZaman/CausalDiagnosticity) | Public repository and data paths inspected; complete release not yet pinned locally | Appropriate explanatory basis with answer held fixed | Universal natural-process ground truth |
| [ProcessBench](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Qwen/ProcessBench) | Public task files verified | Mathematical error localization and correctness controls | Hidden-process faithfulness |
| [C2-Faith](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05167v2) | Paper accessible; official downloadable release not established in this audit | Consistency and coverage comparison | An already available independent causal-label test set |

GRACE deserves the first external detector evaluation. Its test set is selected for difficult verifier disagreements, so its observed prevalence should not be presented as the natural prevalence of unfaithfulness. The previous 40-example sample and shared upstream questions, particularly LogiQA, require overlap checks. A whole-trace judge also needs a frozen step-level adaptation; a test-time redesign would weaken the independence of this check. Exact release hashes and counts are recorded in the [release audit](2026-09-12-external-dataset-release-check.md).

The causal-diagnosticity design is the best complementary addition because it changes what is identifiable, not just the subject matter. FACE-Eval is the next natural check for disclosure and cue transfer. ProcessBench is useful if a detector appears to be following correctness, but adding more mathematics alone will not resolve the dispute between reporting and causal dependence.

**A focused question for the next phase.** On the same examples, can a trace transmit an influence without disclosing it, and can it transmit that influence while making a false claim about its evidence? Which of these properties do existing detectors actually recognize?

The question separates four endpoints:

| Endpoint | Evidence needed | Example of a distinct outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Answer correctness | An independently established answer or executable check | Correct answer with an invented source attribution |
| Disclosure | The actual prompt and explicit acknowledgment criteria | Acknowledgment of a hint followed by a false tool-use claim |
| Truth of a process/source claim | Complete relevant source or execution records | An incorrect answer that truthfully reports a real tool result |
| Interventional dependence | Controlled access to the actual generator and its continuations | A changed answer probability when the supplied trace changes |

This table is a proposed operational separation, not a newly discovered taxonomy. Its value would come from showing empirical dissociations under controlled comparisons and useful out-of-source prediction. Some claims are not identifiable from available records; the instrument must allow insufficient evidence instead of treating absence of a detected contradiction as complete faithfulness.

**Experiment 1: distinguish alternative criteria from judge mistakes.** Reuse the fixed development split to sample cases across task, generator, length, and native annotation reason. Include a representative random component and a separately reported disagreement-enriched component. Independently assess acknowledgment, explicit source/execution claims, and evidence sufficiency using the same legitimate input records for all procedures.

The existing 60-question human packet can support this work. Extend it only with a documented amendment, maintain blindness to native labels and prior scores, and report reviewer agreement and adjudication. Model-produced labels can guide triage but cannot serve as the independent validation of another model's allegations. For acknowledgment-related and explicit-violation subsets, report overlapping membership or prespecified exclusive definitions rather than summing overlapping flags.

If a judge is coherent under a declared alternative rule but disagrees with the native target, that supports a reference-standard explanation on those cases. If it asserts an event contradicted by a complete record, that is an interpretation error. If the decisive record is absent, neither side gains a validated process label. This experiment directly tests the attractive reinterpretation offered in the assessment.

**Experiment 2: hold the answer fixed and vary the evidential basis.** Construct paired contexts with different legitimate routes to the same answer. Generate explanations in each context, retain all inputs and outputs, and test both appropriate and mismatched context–explanation pairings. Start with source-grounded and executable tasks, where selected claims can be independently checked.

Use authored pairs for instrument checks, then naturally generated pairs for the scientific result. Match or randomize source identifiers, length, answer strings, and formatting so those features cannot trivially reveal the label. Retain failed generations and unsuccessful interventions in a construction-flow table. Otherwise selecting only clean separations could recreate the same composition problem that weakened the native pilot.

Primary endpoints should be appropriate-pair ordering and contradiction detection, with false allegations and abstention coverage. Use question-paired uncertainty and hold out source templates. Final-answer correctness is constant within the intended comparison, making an incorrectness oracle uninformative by design. This provides a direct test of a detector's evidence sensitivity without pretending that every aspect of the model's computation is observable.

**Experiment 3: separate disclosure from mediated influence.** On fresh open-weight generations, retain a baseline prompt P0, a hinted prompt P1, and traces C0 and C1 generated under the respective prompts. For each retained pair, evaluate the probability p_ij of the hinted answer under prompt Pi with trace Cj supplied. Run all four combinations rather than inferring mediation from one changed answer.

The trace contrast p_01 − p_00 and the prompt contrast p_10 − p_00 need not sum to p_11 − p_00. The interaction is p_11 − p_10 − p_01 + p_00. Report it, or use a clearly specified decomposition that handles interaction. These are controlled replay measurements; cross-prompt trace insertion can create unnatural inputs, so they are not automatically identified natural mediation effects.

Add paraphrase controls, equally long irrelevant traces, and controls for copying a hinted answer string. Include multiple baseline and treatment generations and inspect effects when the final answer string is removed from the supplied rationale under a separately declared protocol. Assess disclosure and checkable false claims separately. A positive replay effect alone would not show that the trace's semantic argument, rather than an answer cue, drove the output.

Run this as a bounded complementary experiment after the component targets are settled. A large mechanistic branch should not displace the original transfer problem. Its specific purpose is to test whether the alternative-criterion explanation has observable support on common examples.

**Experiment 4: test sampling budget separately from response length.** Use a factorial design with fixed per-response token limits, for example 256/1,024/4,096, and sample counts 1/4/16. These are initial design values, not a power calculation. Record truncation and realized lengths, and include all sampled responses rather than conditioning silently on those that follow the hint.

Report per-response disclosure, behavioral hint adoption, disclosure conditional on adoption, and at-least-one disclosure across attempts as separate quantities. Compare best-of-k changes with the expectation from the observed single-attempt distribution. This can distinguish a practical benefit of repeated querying from a claim that an individual longer explanation reveals more of its influences.

**Experiment 5: replicate the detector comparison and test external transfer.** The full BonaFide 2×2 remains useful: generic/component procedure crossed with restricted/full legitimate prompt. Preserve the existing primary B1−A1 contrast; declare amendments and secondary interactions before scoring. Include the corrected definition-aware comparator where specified. Retain the 807-response partition as reserved from this instrument, while acknowledging that prior full-population analyses informed the hypotheses.

In parallel, freeze a step-level procedure using GRACE development material and evaluate its human test set after overlap checks. Keep input evidence identical across generic and component comparisons. Report native targets separately from shared components, and report detector errors and uncertainty by question rather than treating every step or repeated judge call as independent.

Do not merge benchmark predictions into one headline AUROC. Train or tune on A and evaluate on B only after naming whether the endpoint is the native label or a shared component; report the reverse direction as well. Sparse faithful support within some BonaFide task/generator cells is an information limitation that more calls on the same responses cannot repair.

**The proposed self-hosted panel needs revision.** Qwen3-32B thinking/non-thinking is a useful within-checkpoint mode comparison, with Gemma-3-27B-it providing another family. It yields three configurations across two families, not three independent families. Neither checkpoint is an exact generator in the frozen BonaFide population, but Qwen-family relatedness remains; exact-model exclusion does not eliminate every source of affinity.

Qwen's official card recommends different sampling settings for its modes and warns against greedy decoding in thinking mode. A comparison using recommended settings estimates the effect of the whole deployed configuration. A matched-setting comparison has a different interpretation. Specify both if reasoning-mode attribution matters, and avoid calling the current proposal preregistered. [Qwen3-32B model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-32B).[^6]

Anvil's documented A100 nodes have 40 GB GPUs and its H100 nodes have 80 GB GPUs. Approximately 32.8 billion parameters require about 65.6 GB for two-byte weights alone, before KV cache and runtime memory. A one-A100 BF16 smoke test therefore does not match the proposed 32B setup; use an appropriate H100 allocation, declared tensor parallelism, or a separately recorded quantized configuration. [Anvil architecture](https://docs.rcac.purdue.edu/userguides/anvil/architecture/).[^7]

The estimated 43.2 million input tokens follows arithmetically from 1.2 million per pass times 36 passes, but the per-pass count still needs actual tokenizer and arm-specific verification. Thinking output, context lengths, batching, KV memory, prefix caching, and model loading affect runtime. Measure completed examples and generated tokens per GPU-hour on the actual harness; a prefill-throughput estimate cannot establish the total sweep cost.

An 807-call GPT-4o A1 anchor preserves a historical comparison but cannot estimate that model's within-model 2×2 effect. Use all four arms on a matched subset if that comparison matters. At the listed $2.50 per million input tokens, 1.2 million input tokens cost $3 before output charges; outputs are listed at $10 per million. [Official GPT-4o pricing](https://platform.openai.com/docs/models/gpt-4o).[^8]

The verified allocation does not currently support describing this sweep as nearly free against 1,000 available hours: those figures were allocation limits, and the checked balances are nearly exhausted. A separate local operational snapshot records the balances and storage constraints. This is a resource correction, not a reason to abandon the scientific design or to infer permission to spend another project's allocation.

Begin serving validation with 12–20 development examples covering long inputs, message roles, reasoning output, JSON completion, and truncation. Expand to approximately 150 development examples for throughput and semantic checks before freezing a full run. Record model revisions, engine versions, quantization, seeds, requested and realized token counts, stop reasons, and raw reasoning/final fields. Constrain final JSON without accidentally suppressing the mode being tested. Repeat stochastic draws and backend reproducibility checks as distinct experiments; identical deterministic outputs are not independent samples.

**Paper direction and decision rules.** The immediate priority is three connected activities: the common-example reason audit, a frozen GRACE cross-check, and independently checkable same-answer evidence pairs. The broad judge sweep establishes robustness, but it does not by itself distinguish competing explanations for the failure. The causal replay experiment should answer a targeted remaining question rather than become a second unrelated paper inside the first.

| Outcome | Defensible paper direction |
|---|---|
| Native-label transport fails while independently checked components transfer | An empirical account of reference standards and aggregation limiting transfer |
| Decisive records improve correct allegations across new tasks and generators | A narrowly defined evidence-based verification procedure, compared with relevant existing methods |
| Influence, disclosure, and claim truth reliably dissociate on common examples | An evaluation showing the practical consequences of conflating these endpoints |
| Component gains disappear under repetition or independent checks | A focused measurement and selection audit, without a forced method claim |

The present title and manuscript should not promise any of these outcomes before they are established. The most valuable next evidence is evidence that can distinguish the explanations, even if it eliminates the appealing component-method story. A larger collection of judge scores with no independent target check would leave the central problem unresolved.
